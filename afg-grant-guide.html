How Fire Departments Pay for Training Structures: The AFG Grant Guide

July 2026 · 6 min read · RADCON

Most fire training structures in America are bought with federal money. If your department has been putting off a burn building or training tower because the budget is not there, this guide is for you: how the FEMA Assistance to Firefighters Grant (AFG) program works, what to have ready before the application window opens, and how to spec a container-based training structure that fits a grant budget.

What the AFG program is

The Assistance to Firefighters Grant program provides funding directly to fire departments, EMS organizations, and State Fire Training Academies to equip and train personnel to recognized standards. In the FY2025 cycle, FEMA made $324 million available through AFG. Training facilities and equipment that improve firefighter safety are the heart of what the program funds.

The annual rhythm — and why departments miss it

AFG runs on an annual cycle, and the application window is short. The FY2025 window ran May 19 to June 22, 2026 — roughly a five-week sprint. Departments that win are the ones with specs, quotes, and needs statements ready before the Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO) drops. Departments that start when the window opens usually run out of runway. Check fema.gov/grants/preparedness/firefighters for the current cycle.

What to have ready before the window opens

  1. A needs statement tied to specific training gaps and firefighter safety. NFPA 1403 live-fire training compliance is a strong hook — if your department lacks a compliant structure for live-fire evolutions, say so plainly.
  2. A budgetary quote and spec sheet from your vendor. RADCON provides these free, sized to your department, with no obligation.
  3. A cost-share plan. The federal share varies by the population you serve — verify the current split in the NOFO.
  4. A maintenance and sustainment plan showing the structure will serve for decades.
  5. A current SAM.gov registration. This takes weeks to process — do it first, not last.

Why container-based structures fit grant budgets

A container-based training structure delivers live-fire-capable training at a fraction of the cost of a masonry burn building, installs in days rather than months, and expands in phases as future grant cycles fund. That phased approach matters in an application: it lets your needs statement show a long-term training plan rather than a one-time ask.

Program rules, eligible costs, and cost-share requirements change each cycle. Always verify details against the current NOFO at grants.gov, or call the AFG Help Desk at 866-274-0960.
