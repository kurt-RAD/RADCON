What a Container Home Really Costs in Indiana (2026 Guide)

July 2026 · 8 min read · RADCON

Search "container home cost" and you'll find blogs promising a home for $15,000 to $50,000. Those numbers describe unfinished shells — not a code-compliant, insulated, livable home. This guide gives you the real numbers, from the team that actually welds container homes in Anderson, Indiana.

The honest price range: $150,000 to over $1 million

A real container home from an Indiana fabricator — engineered, insulated to residential code, plumbed, wired, and finished — starts around $150,000 for an efficient single-container or compact build. Multi-container family homes run higher depending on size and finish. Multi-story architectural builds with cantilevers, floor-to-ceiling glass, and rooftop decks reach seven figures. The difference is not the containers; it is everything you dream up around them.

What drives the cost

  • Container count and configuration — each container adds structure, cutting, and reinforcement work
  • Insulation approach — batt or spray foam, both specified to residential building code
  • Glazing — large window and door openings require structural reinforcement
  • Foundation type — pier, slab, or crawl space, driven by your site
  • Site utilities — well, septic or sewer, and electrical service
  • Delivery distance and crane requirements

Are container homes legal in Indiana?

Yes — but it is a county-by-county question. Indiana follows the International Building Code, which added provisions for container construction in 2021. Most municipalities require a building permit, and residential zones vary widely on container dwellings: some counties welcome them, others restrict placement or require special approval, and setback and visibility rules apply in many municipalities. Call your county planning office before you buy land, and expect them to ask for engineered drawings.

Why the permit office loves PE-stamped drawings

This is where most container home dreams die: the county asks for engineering documentation the broker cannot produce. Every RADCON build includes drawings stamped by our licensed Professional Engineer — the document that turns a skeptical plan reviewer into a cooperative one.

Insulation: the Midwest question

Steel conducts heat and cold aggressively, and condensation management is the detail amateur builds get wrong. RADCON insulates with batt or spray foam systems specified to residential building code — engineered into the design from the start, not an afterthought. Spray foam seals the steel envelope and manages condensation best; batts cost less within framed interior walls. We will recommend the right system for your design and budget.

Built indoors, not in the weather

Site-built homes fight rain, mud, and scheduling for months. A RADCON home is welded, insulated, wired, and finished inside our 80,000 square foot Anderson facility — then delivered and craned onto your foundation. Indoor fabrication means tighter tolerances, dry materials, and a schedule the weather cannot touch. For the right customer, we document the build with progress photos throughout.

Ten questions to ask any container home builder

  1. Who actually fabricates my home — you, or a subcontractor I will never meet?
  2. Can I visit the facility where it will be built?
  3. Who stamps the engineering drawings my county will require?
  4. What insulation system do you use, and does it meet residential code?
  5. How do you manage condensation in a steel structure?
  6. What is your welding quality-control process?
  7. Can I see a build in progress?
  8. What does delivery and crane placement cost for my site?
  9. What is the real timeline from deposit to move-in?
  10. Do you build anything else out of containers?

That last question matters more than it sounds. RADCON builds ICC 500 certified tornado shelters rated for 250+ MPH winds and fire training structures that take live-fire punishment. The welds that survive a storm rating are the welds holding your home together.
